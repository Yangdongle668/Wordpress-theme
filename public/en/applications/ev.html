Design challenges

EV engineering is the hardest job in batteries.

We build for the three constraints that dominate every automotive program — and take them seriously, one by one.

  • 01

    Energy density

    Every Wh/kg on the cell compounds into kilometers on the spec sheet — and kilograms off the chassis. Our high-nickel platform lifts volumetric density 18% above the NMC 622 baseline.

  • 02

    Thermal safety

    Passenger vehicles share a compartment with their battery. Ceramic separators, flame-retardant electrolyte, and pack-level venting are baseline for us — not upgrades.

  • 03

    Cycle life

    Real-world duty profiles — fast charge, city stop-and-go, DCFC abuse — differ from the lab. We validate 1,500 cycles to 80% against OEM drive-cycle emulations, not idealized protocols.

How we solve it

One chemistry platform, five automotive variants.

Our cells share a core chemistry, with variants that shift cathode composition, anode doping, and pack geometry to match each vehicle class. The shared platform means validation and supply chain carry across programs.

  • Passenger EV: NMC-811 high-density cells, 220 Wh/kg
  • Commercial vans: LFP prismatic modules, 6,000-cycle life
  • Light EV / e-scooter: high-rate pouch cells, 15C
  • Hybrid buses: mixed chemistry packs for drive + regen
  • Two-wheeler swap: standardized 48 V modules
